## Shared production rules derived from both references

### Character design and acting
- Characters have simple, highly readable round/white faces, compact bodies, robe-like costumes, small accessories/headwear and thin expressive limbs.
- Keep each recurring character's face shape, costume silhouette, colors, scale and accessories locked across all scenes. Do not redesign characters between shots.
- Build facial controls for brows, eyelids, pupils, mouth shapes, cheeks and head tilt. Add body lean, shoulder/arm gestures and readable hand poses.
- Expressions must change in response to dialogue and events. Avoid static faces during speech.
- Use phoneme/viseme-aware lip-sync or a tested approximation tied to actual dialogue audio; do not animate mouth movement from guessed timestamps.

### Environments and props
- Build actual 3D geometry: palace columns, capitals and bases; carved/lattice panels; roof eaves; lanterns; stone paving; carpets; throne; castle walls; gates; workshop benches; blueprints; miniature models; cage bars; pendulum/weight mechanism; dragon-head prop; and terrain/cross-section layers as required by the story.
- Give each story its own new environment design. Reuse character rigs, not the same background for every story.
- Use layered depth: foreground columns/props, midground characters, background architecture/sky/crowds.
- Props that drive the plot must be modeled as recognizable objects and shown in insert shots before/after important actions.

### Lighting and color
- Palace interiors: warm key light, rich red/gold practicals, soft fill, controlled highlights on metallic/gold trim.
- Day exteriors: bright natural key, soft sky fill, readable shadows and saturated but controlled colors.
- Night exteriors: cool blue moon/sky key plus warm lantern pools; keep faces readable.
- Workshop: warm practical overhead lighting, wood bounce and focused task light on the model/workbench.
- Sunset/destruction: warm low-angle light, volumetric haze/smoke only where motivated, controlled exposure.
- Separate scenes through intentional lighting and palette changes, not only by changing background assets.

### Camera and edit rhythm
- Plan a sequence of wide establishing, medium two-shot, close-up reaction, object insert, high/overhead, tracking/side-follow and final reveal shots.
- Use motivated camera movement and preserve screen direction across action.
- Avoid holding one static camera for long dialogue sections; use reaction cutaways and object inserts.
- Camera shake should be brief and tied to a specific impact/reveal only. The visual pass alone does not establish exact shake amplitude or timing; inspect native frames around the event before copying.
- Use foreground elements to create parallax and depth, but do not obscure key action or captions.

### Captions and sound
- Both references show bold, high-contrast, centered lower-screen English captions, often broken into very short word/phrase chunks.
- Implement word-by-word captions from actual speech recognition/forced-alignment timestamps. Highlight the currently spoken word; keep safe margins and avoid covering faces or key props.
- Do not hardcode guessed word timings. Validate caption onset/offset against the audio.
- Mix narration clearly above music and effects; sync impacts, movement, smoke/explosion and prop sounds to the actual action.
